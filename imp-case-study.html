Imposter Syndrome Toolkit

Project Type

Interaction Design

Timeline

September — December 2022

Role

UX Designer & Researcher

Focus

User Research & App Design

1 in 5
college students experience imposter syndrome
Brigham Young University, 2019

Picture a freshman walking into their first computer science lecture. They sit down, pull out their laptop, and as the professor begins explaining algorithms, a familiar wave of doubt washes over them.

Should I be taking notes? Why don't I understand this? Everyone else seems to get it but me.
"Maybe I chose the wrong major."

That freshman was me. And it turns out, I wasn't alone.

Research & Discovery

Through user interviews, literature review, and competitive analysis, I sought to understand how college students navigate feelings of inadequacy and self-doubt.

User Interviews

In-depth conversations with college students experiencing imposter syndrome to understand their personal struggles and coping mechanisms.

Literature Review

Analysis of academic research on imposter syndrome to build upon established psychological findings and identify key patterns.

Competitive Analysis

Evaluation of existing mental health and wellness apps to identify gaps in current solutions and opportunities for innovation.

Key Findings

Communication is Key

What seems to help best is any form of communication regarding everyone's individual situations. Listening to and discussing imposter syndrome with others is critical for healing.

Importance of Belonging

Everyone wants to feel like they belong in a space. When that doesn't happen, it can make students feel deeply uncomfortable and question their place.

Internet as a Tool

Platforms like Reddit and Quora are the most helpful online advice sources. Users know they'll find someone in a similar situation with comforting replies.

Design Evolution

First Iteration: Safe Spaces

An app to find safe spaces on campus where users could look through inclusive events and clubs to connect with others going through similar experiences.

First Iteration Wireframes

Safe Spaces app: Home, Events, and Mentorship screens
Shortcomings Discovered

Despite being helpful for connecting students with similar interests, this approach wasn't direct enough. Research showed students wanted immediate advice and comfort at their fingertips to tackle these emotions in real-time.

User testing revealed that individuals wanted a hub full of resources to guide them through their emotions rather than just social connections. Instead of a professional or sterile atmosphere, the app should focus on softness and make users feel comforted. This feedback was crucial in pivoting from a social approach to a personal resource toolkit.

Low-Fidelity Exploration

Early wireframes exploring the toolkit concept with basic mood tracking, resource access, and user-friendly interactions.

Low-Fidelity Wireframes

Initial toolkit concept: Welcome, Check-In, and Resources screens

Refined Problem Statement

As a college student, I want to have easy access to various resources so I can reassure and guide myself through feelings of imposter syndrome.

Final Solution

Design Philosophy

Taking inspiration from meditation apps, I focused on making everything legible, clearly labeled, and calm. Since users are already overwhelmed, the design needed to provide comfort rather than add stress.

High-Fidelity Prototypes

Final design: Login, Daily Check-In, Resources Hub, and Affirmations

Impact & Key Learnings

Design Impact

Created a solution that directly addresses the root need: immediate, accessible emotional support rather than indirect social connections.

Research Insights

User feedback was crucial in pivoting from a social approach to a personal resource toolkit, validating the importance of iterative design.

Personal Growth

I learned that my initial ideas don't always stick and that's okay. Letting research and user feedback guide design decisions, rather than clinging to original concepts, leads to more meaningful solutions.
